// 04 — manifest

A love letter to the demoscene.

What is the demoscene? A computer art subculture that grew out of the cracktros of the 1980s: the animated intros crackers stuck on pirated games to sign their work. Soon people made them for their own sake. A demo is a program that plays an audiovisual show in real time: no video file, just code, math and music, often squeezed into a few kilobytes and judged at parties by other coders and artists. Read more on Wikipedia →

I have always been fascinated by computer generated imagery, and to me the demoscene is its highest form: the place where code meets art. A demo is never a recording. It exists for a fraction of a second at a time, in your GPU’s memory, and then it is gone and drawn again.

Yes, AI helped to make this: the music, the sprites and much of the code. It is still my love letter to everyone who pioneered this scene, and everyone who keeps it alive. Nothing was cracked here. The only thing that got hacked is a perfectly good synthwave sunset.

— hodiloves

05 — f.a.q.4 entries
Is it a virus?

No. It is an unsigned PyInstaller exe, which some antivirus tools and Windows SmartScreen flag by reflex. Every line of source is in the repo, the build is a single script, and each release lists the SHA-256 of the exe. Don't trust it? Build it yourself, or run it from source.

What do I need?

Windows 10/11 and a GPU with OpenGL 3.3 (anything from the last decade). It adapts to your screen: 16:9, ultrawide, even a rotated monitor. Slow GPU? Press - or start it with --scale 0.5.

Controls?

Esc quit · Space pause · ← → seek ±10s · R restart · F fullscreen · F12 screenshot · - = render scale. In the credits press 1-4 to open the links.

Can I render it to a video?

Yes. R00T_ACCS.exe --export out.mp4 --res 1920x1080 (needs ffmpeg on PATH). Use --res 1080x1920 for the vertical cut. The picture is a pure function of time, so exports are frame-exact.
